# 🚀 Sprint 6: Energy & Manipulability-Aware Trajectory Optimization

## 📌 Overview

This sprint focuses on enabling the robot to **choose how to move**, not just follow a predefined path.

Instead of executing a single trajectory, multiple candidate trajectories are generated and evaluated based on:

- Energy consumption
- Manipulability (distance from singularities)
- Constraint feasibility

An **adaptive sampling strategy** is then applied to refine the solution and obtain an optimal trajectory.

---

## 🎯 Objective

Develop a trajectory selection framework that:

- Minimizes energy consumption
- Avoids singular configurations
- Respects physical constraints
- Improves trajectory quality through adaptive refinement

---

## 🧠 Methodology

### 1. Trajectory Generation

Two modes are used:

#### Fixed Mode
Predefined trajectories:
- Straight line  
- Arc  
- Smooth spline  

#### Random Mode
- Generate multiple trajectories using **quadratic Bézier curves**
- Introduce randomness via control point sampling

Trajectory equation:

`x(t) = (1 - τ)^2 P0 + 2(1 - τ)τ P1 + τ^2 P2`

where:
- `P0` = start position  
- `P1` = control point  
- `P2` = goal position  
- `τ = t / T`, with `τ ∈ [0, 1]`

---

### 2. Simulation-Based Evaluation

Each trajectory is executed in the Simulink model:

- Joint torques `τ(t)`
- End-effector motion
- Energy consumption
- Manipulability index

---

### 3. Constraint Filtering

Trajectories are rejected if:

- Manipulability falls below threshold  
  `w < 0.0015`

- Torque exceeds limit  
  `|τ| > 150`

Rejected trajectories are excluded from optimization.

---

### 4. Cost Function

A combined cost is defined:

`J = ∫ ( |P(t)| + λ * (1 / w(t)) ) dt`

where:
- `P(t) = dE/dt` (power)
- `w(t)` = manipulability
- `λ` = weighting factor

This balances:
- Energy efficiency
- Distance from singularity

---

### 5. Adaptive Sampling

After initial evaluation:

- The best trajectory (based on cost) is selected
- Its control point is extracted
- New trajectories are generated by adding small perturbations:

`P1_new = P1_best + noise`

This enables:
- Local refinement
- Faster convergence to optimal solution

---

## 📊 Results

Two optimization criteria are compared:

### Energy-Only Optimization
- Minimizes energy consumption
- May pass through low manipulability regions

### Energy + Manipulability Optimization
- Produces safer and more stable trajectories
- Avoids singularities
- Slightly higher energy but more practical

## 🔍 Key Insights

- Energy-optimal trajectories are not always physically safe  
- Incorporating manipulability improves robustness  
- Adaptive sampling significantly improves solution quality  
- Control-point-based refinement is effective for trajectory optimization  

---

## 🏁 Conclusion

This sprint transforms the system from:

**Path following → Path optimization**

The robot now:
- Evaluates multiple motion strategies
- Filters unsafe trajectories
- Selects optimal motion based on performance metrics
- Refines solutions using adaptive sampling
